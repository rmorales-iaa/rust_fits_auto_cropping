# Scientific Photometry

Respond in English. Preserve valid pixel values and units. Model sky background robustly while excluding sources, saturation and invalid samples. Distinguish background structure from instrumental artifacts; do not remove faint emission merely because it is smooth or correlated. For this clipping tool, use pixel-derived candidates only and treat structural/scaling/null cards as decoding metadata, not photometric evidence. Compare aperture fluxes, sky estimates and uncertainties before/after processing on unmasked sources; include edge stars, extended objects, PSF wings, gradients and faint injected sources. Crop mode selects the global maximum-area clean axis-aligned rectangle over detector exclusions and originally undefined samples, without circle constraints. Explicitly count defined pixels sacrificed versus artifact pixels removed, verify `NAXIS1/2` and apply the configured utility threshold; if rejected, keep the source via symlink without modifying it. A circle-like field can lose more pixels under the rectangular product contract. Track masked fractions for source and background apertures and flag incomplete measurements. Do not replace rejected values with apparently valid zeros. Quantify selection bias and document uncertainty and evidence in `input/doc/`.

Call unlabeled pixels suspected artifacts or unclassified sacrificed pixels; they are not ground-truth unusable/valid areas. Separate original undefined samples from new loss. Measure lost detections as well as the fluxes of survivors, and compare backgrounds under independently specified aperture/annulus policies. Source masks protect estimator fits and decision constraints; they do not authorize deleting sources or interpolating their flux. Freeze source-loss and completeness budgets before tuning a mask-versus-crop policy.
